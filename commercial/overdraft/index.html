Frequently asked questions.

How does a business overdraft work?

A business overdraft is a pre-approved credit limit attached to your business bank account. When your account balance goes below zero, the overdraft kicks in automatically. You only pay interest on the amount you've drawn, and as money comes back into your account, the drawn amount reduces. It's a revolving facility, so you can draw and repay as often as needed.

What is the difference between a secured and unsecured business overdraft?

A secured overdraft uses an asset (typically property) as collateral, which allows the lender to offer a higher limit and lower interest rate. An unsecured overdraft has no collateral requirement but typically comes with a lower limit and higher rate. Your Compare Report will show both options based on your situation.

What does a business overdraft cost?

Interest rates on business overdrafts typically start from around 8% p.a. for secured facilities with major banks, up to 15 to 20% p.a. for unsecured facilities. Most lenders also charge a monthly or annual line fee, around 0.5 to 1.5% of the facility limit. You only pay interest on the amount actually drawn.

What is the difference between an overdraft and a line of credit?

They're very similar. Both are revolving credit facilities where you pay interest only on drawn funds. The main difference is that an overdraft is attached to your everyday business bank account and activates automatically when your balance goes negative, while a line of credit is usually a separate account you draw from intentionally. Some lenders use the terms interchangeably.
