Frequently asked questions.

What is invoice finance?

Invoice finance is an ongoing facility that lets you access cash tied up in unpaid invoices. Instead of waiting 30, 60, or 90 days for customers to pay, a lender advances you up to 90% of the invoice value, typically within 24 hours, with the balance paid once your customer settles.

How is invoice finance different from Fund an Invoice?

Invoice finance is an ongoing, revolving facility across your entire debtor ledger, whereas Fund an Invoice lets you finance a single invoice at a time with no ongoing commitment. Invoice finance typically has lower per-invoice rates but requires whole-of-ledger assignment.

What is the difference between invoice discounting and factoring?

With invoice discounting, you retain control of your sales ledger and your customers are unaware of the arrangement. With factoring, the lender manages your debtor ledger and collects payments directly from your customers.

How much does invoice finance cost?

Typical fees range from 1% to 3% of the invoice value per 30 days for specialist lenders, and 0.5% to 1.5% per 30 days for bank lenders, depending on your industry, debtor quality, and volume. There may also be service or establishment fees.

What happens if my customer doesn’t pay?

With recourse factoring (the most common type), you’re responsible for repaying the advance if the debtor defaults. With non-recourse factoring, the lender absorbs the credit risk, but this typically comes at a higher cost.

Can startups use invoice finance?

Yes. Some lenders offer invoice finance to businesses with as little as 3 months trading history. The key factor is the quality and creditworthiness of your debtors, not the age of your business.
