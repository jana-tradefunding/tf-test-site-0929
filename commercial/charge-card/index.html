Frequently asked questions.

What is the difference between a charge card and a credit card?

A charge card requires you to pay the full balance each billing cycle, typically 30 to 55 days, while a credit card allows you to carry a balance and pay interest. Charge cards offer interest-free float but no option to revolve debt, which suits businesses that want working capital flexibility without the cost of carrying a balance.

What credit limits are available on business charge cards?

Limits typically range from $5,000 to $500,000 depending on the provider and your business profile. Some providers offer higher limits for established businesses with stronger revenue. Your Compare Report shows what each provider offers based on your revenue and credit profile.

Who qualifies for a business charge card?

Most providers require an active ABN, at least 6 months of trading history, and monthly revenue from around $5,000 upward, with better fees and higher limits available above $100,000 in annual revenue. Personal credit for the director is also assessed. Newer fintech providers can be more flexible on requirements.

How is a charge card different from a line of credit?

A line of credit transfers cash to your bank account for any business purpose, including payroll, rent, and supplier payments, and charges interest on the drawn balance. A charge card is a payment instrument for purchases only, must typically be paid in full each cycle, and carries no ongoing interest when paid on time.

Can I use a charge card for expense management?

Yes. Many business charge cards include employee sub-cards with individual spending limits, real-time transaction notifications, receipt capture, and accounting software integration, making them a common choice for teams that need shared spending control.
