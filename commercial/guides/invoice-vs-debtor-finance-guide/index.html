Invoice finance vs. debtor finance: the difference, and which fits.

Invoice Finance vs. Debtor Finance: The Core Difference

Invoice finance - also called spot factoring - funds a single invoice, one transaction at a time. You choose which invoice to fund, the financier advances a percentage of its face value, typically within 24 to 48 hours, and the facility closes out the moment that invoice is paid. There's no ongoing commitment: fund one invoice this month and none next month if that's what suits your cash flow.

Debtor finance - sometimes called invoice factoring or a receivables facility when set up on a whole-ledger basis - is a revolving line secured against your entire debtor book, or an agreed segment of it. Every eligible invoice you raise is automatically drawn against the facility, funds are advanced continuously as new invoices are issued, and the limit grows and shrinks with your ledger. It behaves more like a business line of credit than a one-off transaction, with a blended monthly rate applied across the whole book rather than a fee calculated per invoice.

Worked Example: The Same $50,000 Invoice, Two Structures

A $50,000 Invoice, Paid in 35 Days

Same invoice, same customer, same 35-day payment term - funded two different ways. Both advance 85% ($42,500) on day one. The difference shows up in the fee charged on the remaining 15% held back until the customer pays.

SPOT INVOICE FINANCE
2.4% per 30 days
Advance (85%): $42,500 upfront
Fee: 2.4% × ~1.17 periods = $1,428
Net proceeds: $48,572
Held on account: $0 after payout
DEBTOR FINANCE FACILITY
1.3% per 30 days (blended)
Advance (85%): $42,500 upfront
Fee: 1.3% × ~1.17 periods = $774
Net proceeds: $49,226
Plus: monthly facility fee applies across whole ledger

On this single invoice, the debtor finance facility nets $654 more - but that comparison only holds once your whole ledger is running through it, not one invoice in isolation. A facility fee applies whether or not you draw against every invoice, which is the trade-off for the lower per-invoice rate.

Disclosed vs. Undisclosed Facilities

This is the decision most business owners weigh hardest, and it's independent of whether you choose invoice finance or debtor finance - both structures are available either way.

Disclosed
Your customer is notified a financier is involved and pays funds directly into the financier's trust account. Typically cheaper, faster to set up, and standard for most non-bank facilities in Australia.
Undisclosed
Your customer keeps paying you as normal; you then remit to the financier. Preserves the appearance of self-funding but usually carries a rate premium and tighter reporting requirements.
Why choose disclosed
Lower cost, simpler collections, and increasingly normal - many customers, especially larger corporates, are used to paying into a financier's account and don't read it as a signal of financial stress.
Why choose undisclosed
Businesses with sensitive customer relationships, or in sectors where financing arrangements could affect negotiating position, often pay the premium to keep the arrangement private.

Eligibility & Documents

Both structures are assessed primarily on the quality of your debtors, ahead of your own business - a meaningful difference from a term loan, where the lender is mostly assessing you. Debtor concentration matters more here than on almost any other product: a ledger with one customer accounting for 60% of revenue will be funded more cautiously than one spread across a dozen reliable payers, even if total revenue is identical.

Debtor Quality
Financiers check the customers you invoice as closely as they check you - a slow-paying or high-risk debtor can be excluded from the facility even if the rest of your ledger qualifies.
Debtor Concentration
Most financiers cap any single customer at 20 to 30% of the funded ledger; above that, expect a lower advance rate or exclusion on the excess.
Invoice Terms
30 to 90 day trade terms are the sweet spot; invoices due in under 14 days are rarely worth funding once fees are applied.
Time Trading
6+ months typical for non-bank; debtor finance facilities from bank-affiliated financiers often want 12+ months.

Choosing Between the Two

CHOOSE INVOICE FINANCE WHEN
You only have one or two specific invoices needing early cash
No ongoing commitment or facility fee - pay only for what you fund
Faster to set up the first time, useful for testing the product
You want to keep the rest of your ledger untouched
CHOOSE DEBTOR FINANCE WHEN
You fund invoices most months, not occasionally
Lower blended rate outweighs the facility fee at your volume
You need funds to draw automatically as new invoices are raised
Facility fee applies whether you draw fully or not

Real-World Case Study

MELBOURNE WHOLESALE DISTRIBUTION

A Melbourne wholesale distributor supplying independent grocers on 60-day terms had a specific problem: payroll was due in eight days, and $64,000 was sitting in a single invoice to their largest customer that wasn't due for another five weeks. They considered two paths. The first was a one-off spot invoice advance on that single invoice - 85% ($54,400) released within 24 hours at 2.6% per 30 days, covering payroll with no change to the rest of their operations. The second was moving their whole ledger onto an ongoing debtor finance facility, given that 60-day terms were the norm across most of their customer base, not an exception created by this one invoice.

They ran the spot advance to fix the immediate payroll gap, then reviewed their full ledger over the following month. With roughly $280,000 in receivables outstanding at any given time and invoices funded most months, the blended debtor finance rate worked out cheaper than repeated spot advances would have - so they moved onto a $220,000 revolving facility three weeks later, disclosed, covering their five largest customers. The spot advance solved the emergency; the facility solved the recurring pattern behind it.

Frequently Asked Questions

Will my customers know I'm using invoice finance?

Only if the facility is disclosed, which most non-bank facilities are by default. Your customer would see payment instructions directing funds to the financier's trust account. Undisclosed facilities exist for businesses that want to keep the arrangement private, usually at a rate premium.

What happens if my customer pays late?

Under a disclosed facility, the financier typically manages follow-up directly. Fees usually accrue for the extra days the invoice remains unpaid, so a late payment increases the cost slightly rather than defaulting the facility - provided the customer eventually pays.

Can I finance just one invoice, or do I have to finance my whole ledger?

Both options exist. Spot invoice finance lets you fund a single invoice with no ongoing commitment. Debtor finance requires an agreed portion of your ledger - often your largest or most reliable customers - to run through the facility continuously.

What if my customer doesn't pay at all?

This depends on whether the facility is "with recourse" or "without recourse." Recourse facilities, which are more common and cheaper, mean you're liable to repay the advance if the customer defaults. Non-recourse facilities shift that credit risk to the financier at a higher cost.

Does invoice finance affect my relationship with customers?

Rarely, in practice. Disclosed facilities are common enough in Australian B2B trade that most corporate customers don't treat a change of payment instructions as a red flag. Undisclosed facilities avoid the question entirely by keeping payment flows unchanged.

Can new businesses use debtor finance?

Yes, though most financiers want at least 6 months of trading history and an established set of customers to assess debtor quality against. Newer businesses are more commonly directed toward spot invoice finance first, then offered a facility once a payment pattern is established.

How is this different to a business loan?

A business loan is assessed against your business's overall financial position and repaid on a fixed schedule regardless of when your customers pay you. Invoice and debtor finance are assessed against your debtors' payment reliability and repay themselves automatically as invoices are collected - the funding scales with your receivables rather than sitting as a fixed debt on your balance sheet.

Can I switch from spot invoice finance to a full debtor finance facility later?

Yes - this is a common progression. Many businesses start with occasional spot advances to test the product, then move to an ongoing facility once they recognise a recurring pattern of receivables timing gaps, as in the case study above.
