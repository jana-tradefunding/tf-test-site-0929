What a "Good" Offer Actually Looks Like

Most business owners compare loan offers the way they'd compare two prices on a shelf - whichever number is lower wins. Commercial lending doesn't work that way. Two offers with the same headline rate can differ by tens of thousands of dollars once fees, security requirements, and repayment structure are factored in, and the offer that looks cheapest on the page is very often not the offer that costs the least.

A good offer is one where every term is legible before you sign: the true annualised cost, exactly what's secured against the facility, how much flexibility you have if your circumstances change, and what it costs to get out early. An expensive offer usually isn't expensive because the headline rate was hidden - it's expensive because everything around the rate was left unexamined.

The True Cost of a Loan: APR vs. Headline Rate

This isn't a term-loan-specific problem - it applies to every product in the table above. Any facility with an origination fee, facility fee, or line fee will have a real cost higher than its advertised rate, and the gap widens the shorter the term. Worked example below uses a $150,000 facility over a 12-month term, but the same maths applies whether you're comparing term loans, lines of credit, or equipment finance.

OFFER A
7.9% p.a.
Origination fee: 4% ($6,000)
Facility fee: $50/month ($600/yr)
Total cost: $18,450
Effective rate: ≈12.3%
OFFER B
9.2% p.a.
Origination fee: 1% ($1,500)
Facility fee: None
Total cost: $15,300
Effective rate: ≈10.2%

Offer B carries the higher headline rate but costs $3,150 less over the year - the lesson holds regardless of which product you're comparing.

6 Red Flags to Check Before You Sign

Every one of these sits outside the headline rate, which is exactly why they're easy to miss when comparing offers side by side on price alone.

Origination Fees
A one-off fee deducted from the funded amount or added to the balance - ranges from 0% to 5% and moves the effective rate more on short terms.
Exit & Break Fees
Fixed-rate facilities often carry a break cost calculated against remaining interest; some non-bank lenders charge a flat early-exit fee instead.
PPSR Registration
A registered security interest on the Personal Property Securities Register can sit against specific assets or the whole business - check the scope of it, not simply whether one exists.
Personal Guarantee Scope
Most facilities require a director's guarantee, but the scope varies - some are capped to the facility limit, others extend further. Read the clause itself, not simply that it's required.
Rate-Lock vs. Floating
A variable rate can move with the lender's base rate over the term. Confirm whether your quoted rate is fixed for the full term or subject to review.
Prepayment Penalties
Paying a facility off early should usually save you interest - confirm the lender doesn't charge a penalty that offsets or exceeds that saving.

The S.T.A.R. Framework: A Mental Model for Any Offer

Rather than comparing offers line by line, S.T.A.R. gives you four questions to ask of any facility, on any product, from any lender.

S
Security
What's actually secured - the asset, the business via a general security agreement, or nothing at all - and does that match what you're comfortable putting at risk?
T
Term
Does the repayment term match the life of what you're funding, and can you exit or refinance before it ends without a costly penalty?
A
Amount
Is the amount sized to your actual need and your ability to repay from cash flow - rather than simply the maximum a lender is willing to approve?
R
Rate
What's the true APR once every fee is included - not the number printed largest on the offer document?

Frequently Asked Questions

What's the difference between comparing and applying?

Comparing means reviewing indicative offers based on the information you provide, usually via a soft credit check that doesn't affect your credit file. Applying means submitting a formal application to one specific lender, which triggers a hard credit inquiry. Comparing lets you see the field before committing to any one application.

Does getting quotes hurt my credit score?

No, not when it's done through a soft credit check comparison. Soft checks are visible only to you, not to other lenders, and don't affect your credit score. Only a formal loan application, which involves a hard inquiry, has the potential to affect your file - and even then, typically only if you make several in a short period.

How many lenders should I compare?

There's no fixed number, but comparing across at least a handful of lender tiers - bank, second-tier, fintech, and specialist non-bank where relevant - gives you a realistic view of where your business actually sits. A single quote tells you what one lender will offer; it doesn't tell you what's available.

What if my bank already made me an offer?

Treat it as a starting point, not a ceiling. Banks price to their own risk appetite and existing relationship, which doesn't always produce the sharpest rate or the most flexible terms. Comparing it against other lenders costs nothing and either confirms you already have a good deal or surfaces a better one.

Can I compare secured and unsecured offers side by side?

Yes, but compare them on true APR and total cost, not rate alone. A secured offer will almost always show a lower headline rate; whether that's actually the better deal depends on what you're putting up as security and how that trades off against the rate saving.

How long does a comparison take?

A Compare Report, matched across 100+ products from our panel of 70+ lenders, is typically ready within 24 hours of submitting your business details. There's no waiting on individual lender responses one at a time.

Is comparing free?

Yes. Comparison through Trade Funding carries no cost and no obligation to proceed with any offer surfaced. You only incur costs, and only from the lender you choose, if and when you formally apply and settle a facility.

Will comparing offers delay my funding if I need cash quickly?

Not materially. Because the comparison step uses a soft check and runs in parallel across the panel, it typically adds hours, not days, to the process - and it's common for applicants to move from comparison straight into a formal application with the lender they choose the same day.
