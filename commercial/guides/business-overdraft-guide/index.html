What Is a Business Overdraft?

A business overdraft is an approved limit attached directly to your everyday transaction account, letting the account balance run negative up to that limit without a payment bouncing or a facility declining. There's no separate loan account, no drawdown request, and no fixed repayment date - the account simply moves into and out of overdraft as money comes in and goes out.

Interest accrues only on the negative balance, calculated daily and typically debited to the account monthly. If your account sits at $0 for three weeks and then dips to $12,000 negative for four days before a payment lands, you pay interest on that $12,000 for four days - not on your $40,000 limit for the whole month. That daily-balance mechanic is the single most misunderstood part of how an overdraft actually costs money, and it's the reason an overdraft can be a genuinely cheap way to smooth short cash flow gaps despite carrying a headline rate above a secured term loan.

What a $40,000 Overdraft Actually Costs

Most business owners assume an overdraft limit gets charged like a loan for the full amount, all month. It doesn't. Worked example: a $40,000 approved limit, used for an average of 12 days a month, at a typical second-tier rate of 12.5% p.a.

IF CHARGED ON THE FULL LIMIT
$416.67/mo
$40,000 × 12.5% ÷ 12
This is not how it works
Common misconception only
ACTUAL DAILY-BALANCE COST
$164.38/mo
$40,000 × 12.5% ÷ 365 × 12 days
Total cost: $164.38
Charged only on days drawn

The real monthly cost is roughly 60% lower than the "full limit" assumption - because interest only accrues on the balance and days actually used.

How Much Can You Borrow?

Overdraft limits are sized against how the account actually behaves, rather than a stated funding need alone. Lenders weigh six factors:

Average Account Turnover
Monthly deposits and withdrawals through the account set the ceiling more than annual revenue alone.
Security Offered
Property or asset backing lifts the limit well beyond the unsecured band.
Banking Relationship Length
An established transaction account history with the lender streamlines approval and can raise the limit.
Cash Flow Seasonality
Predictable seasonal dips support a case for a larger buffer than flat, steady trading.
Existing Facilities
Other drawn facilities reduce serviceability (your ability to repay based on cash flow), and so the limit a lender will extend.
Credit Score
A clean file opens the bank tier; defaults or late payments narrow options to non-bank lenders.

Pros & Cons of a Business Overdraft

ADVANTAGES
Interest charged only on what you actually draw, only for the days you draw it
No separate account or drawdown paperwork - funds are already where you bank
Reusable indefinitely once repaid, unlike a lump-sum loan
Fast to arrange against an existing banking relationship
DISADVANTAGES
Limit can be reduced or recalled at annual review
Not designed for large, one-off funding needs
Director guarantees (the director personally backs the debt) typically required

Real-World Case Study

REGIONAL NSW RETAIL & HOSPITALITY

A homewares and café business in Orange, NSW, traded strongly through the November to January peak but ran consistently tight through June and July, when foot traffic and takings both dropped. Rather than take out a term loan sized for the whole year, the owner arranged a $35,000 overdraft against their existing everyday account with a regional bank at 12.9% p.a. The limit was drawn on for an average of nine days each winter month, covering supplier payments and payroll timing gaps until warmer-season trade picked back up. Total interest cost across the two slow months came to just under $220 - a fraction of what a term loan of the same size would have cost carrying a full balance from day one, and the facility simply sat unused and fee-free through the busy months.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need a new bank account to get an overdraft?

Not necessarily. Many lenders will attach an overdraft to your existing transaction account, particularly if you already bank with them. Switching or opening a new account is sometimes required if you're moving to a different lender for a better rate.

Is interest charged on the full limit or just what I use?

Just what you use. Interest accrues daily on the negative balance only, for the days the account is actually in overdraft - not on the approved limit itself. An unused limit costs nothing beyond any annual line fee the lender charges.

Can I get an overdraft with a non-bank lender?

Yes. Fintech and specialist non-bank lenders offer overdraft-style facilities, typically at a higher rate than the major banks but with faster approval and less emphasis on an existing banking relationship.

What happens if I exceed my overdraft limit?

Exceeding the approved limit usually triggers a higher penalty interest rate on the excess, and repeated breaches can lead to the lender reviewing or reducing the facility. It's treated differently to simply using the limit as approved.

Is an overdraft reviewed annually?

Most business overdrafts are reviewed annually, when the lender reassesses account turnover, conduct, and financial position before renewing, adjusting, or in rare cases withdrawing the limit.

How is an overdraft different to a line of credit?

An overdraft sits on your everyday transaction account with no separate account number. A line of credit is a standalone revolving facility with its own account, typically supporting larger and longer draws than an overdraft is designed for.

Can a new business get an overdraft?

It's harder, but not impossible. Most bank lenders want 12+ months of trading history; some non-bank lenders will consider a business with 6 months of consistent account turnover, particularly with director guarantees or additional security.

Will an overdraft affect my credit score?

Applying for an overdraft involves a credit check like any other facility, and the approved limit shows on your credit file as an available line of credit. Using it within the limit and repaying as agreed doesn't damage your score; regular breaches or missed reviews can.
