What Is a Business Term Loan?

A business term loan provides a lump sum upfront, repaid through fixed instalments over a set period - typically 3 months to 7 years, depending on the lender and loan size. Repayments follow an amortisation schedule: each instalment covers a portion of principal and interest, calculated so the loan is fully repaid by the end of the term.

Unlike a line of credit or overdraft, a term loan is a one-off draw rather than a revolving facility. That structure suits a single, defined use - buying equipment, funding an expansion, or consolidating existing debt - rather than ongoing working capital needs.

Understanding the True Cost: APR vs. Headline Rate

A lower headline rate doesn't always mean a cheaper loan. Fees change the real cost more than most borrowers expect - especially on shorter terms, where a one-off origination fee is spread across fewer repayments. Worked example: $150,000 loan, 12-month term.

LENDER A
7.9% p.a.
Origination fee: 4% ($6,000)
Facility fee: $50/month ($600/yr)
Total cost: $18,450
Effective rate: ≈12.3%
LENDER B
9.2% p.a.
Origination fee: 1% ($1,500)
Facility fee: None
Total cost: $15,300
Effective rate: ≈10.2%

Lender B carries the higher headline rate but costs $3,150 less over the year.

How Much Can You Borrow?

Borrowing capacity is sized against the business itself, rather than the amount requested. Lenders weigh six factors:

Annual Revenue
Most unsecured offers cap at 10 to 20% of annual turnover.
Security Offered
Property or asset backing raises the ceiling well beyond the unsecured range.
Time in Business
12+ months trading opens the full panel; under 6 months narrows it to specialist lenders.
Cash Flow Trend
Lenders read 3 to 6 months of bank statements for stability, beyond volume alone.
Existing Debt Load
Existing facilities reduce serviceability (your ability to repay based on cash flow) for a new loan.
Credit Score
A clean file broadens the panel; defaults or late payments narrow it to non-bank options.

Frequently Asked Questions

What's the minimum revenue to qualify for a business term loan?

Most lenders set a minimum of $75,000 to $100,000 in annual revenue for an unsecured facility. Bank lenders typically require higher, established turnover; some specialist non-bank lenders will consider newer businesses with lower revenue against strong cash flow.

Do I need residential property to secure a loan?

No. Unsecured term loans are available up to roughly $500,000 based on business cash flow alone. Residential property security can unlock larger amounts and lower rates, but it isn't required for most standard facilities.

Can I get a term loan with bad credit?

Yes, through specialist non-bank and private lenders - at a higher rate than the bank tier. A default or late payment history narrows the panel rather than closing it entirely.

Does comparing lenders affect my credit score?

Comparing rate estimates through a broker or comparison service typically uses a soft credit check, with no credit score impact. Only a formal application triggers a hard inquiry - this is why comparing before applying protects your file from multiple hard inquiries.

How long does approval take?

Unsecured fintech and non-bank facilities are commonly approved and funded within 24 to 48 hours. Bank and secured facilities take longer - typically 1 to 3 weeks - due to valuation and formal credit assessment requirements.

Can I repay a term loan early?

Most lenders allow early repayment. Fixed-rate bank facilities may carry a break cost calculated against the remaining interest; fintech and non-bank lenders more commonly allow early payout with a discount on unearned interest instead of a penalty.

What's the difference between a term loan and a line of credit?

A term loan disburses a lump sum upfront with a fixed repayment schedule. A line of credit is a revolving facility - you draw funds as needed and pay interest only on the drawn balance, better suited to ongoing working capital than a single funding need.

Will I need to provide a personal guarantee?

Most lenders require a director's guarantee, regardless of whether the loan itself is secured or unsecured. It's a guarantee tied to the director, not additional collateral on the business assets.
