Frequently asked questions.

What is the difference between a finance lease and an operating lease?

The key difference is ownership. With a finance lease, you have the option, and usually the intention, to own the asset at the end of the term by paying a residual. The asset appears on your balance sheet as both an asset and a liability. With an operating lease, you simply use the asset and return it at the end of the term, with no ownership transfer.

What is a balloon or residual payment?

A balloon payment, also called a residual value, is a lump sum due at the end of the finance lease term. Setting a balloon reduces your regular rental payments during the lease. Balloon percentages typically range from 10% to 50% of the asset value. A higher balloon means lower monthly rentals but a larger lump sum at the end.

How is a finance lease treated for tax purposes?

You can generally claim tax deductions for depreciation of the asset and the interest component of the lease payments. Unlike a chattel mortgage, you cannot claim the GST credit upfront, as GST is included in each lease payment. Always consult your accountant for advice specific to your situation.

What happens at the end of a finance lease?

You typically have three options. Pay the residual amount and take full ownership, which is the most common choice. Refinance the residual over an additional period. Or return the asset to the lender, though this is less common since the intention of a finance lease is usually ownership.
